About

Behavioural science
meets real-world AI.

I am a researcher and analyst with a background in behavioural economics, cognitive psychology, and data science. For nearly three years I have worked at the Muthukrishna Lab at LSE, contributing to UNDP research on AI and human development, including the 2025 Human Development Report and the AI4HD Dashboard.

My dissertation examined algorithmic aversion in financial decision-making, asking who avoids delegating to algorithms in real, incentivised investment choices, and through which psychological mechanisms. I am also a published co-author in the Journal of Human Development and Capabilities.

I am a Nova Talent member, IE High Potential Award recipient, and hold two Academic Excellence Awards from LSE in Economics and Psychology.

BSc Dissertation · LSE · 2026
Who Avoids Algorithmic Delegation in Finance? Financial Knowledge and the Limits of Overconfidence in Robo-Advisor Adoption
Behavioural Economics · Algorithmic Aversion · Financial Decision-Making
Using secondary data from two pre-registered incentivised experiments (N = 1,448), this dissertation asks who avoids delegating to robo-advisors and why. The central finding: overconfidence had no effect on adoption. Financial literacy was the operative construct, with both objective and subjective knowledge independently predicting higher adoption. Structural factors, including age and gender, outperformed all psychological predictors.
